Why this particular substep?

It separates all three roles. Gravity predicts a deeply penetrating trial. The two force iterations change that trial; DAT advances the anchor independently at initialization and after each iteration. Two additional final rounds advance the anchor toward the same trial before committing it.

The final rounds need magnification. The anchor is already very close to the triangle after iteration 2. Their small but real advances are visible in the micrometer view.

This is a schedule demonstration. The intentionally low iteration count is not a convergence recommendation. The triangle’s normal is constant, so this example does not show how changing plane normals can influence later contact forces.

Reproduce the recorded calculation

Run from the newton-collision checkout on donglaix/rigid-soft-alm-dat-design, commit . The script uses the real CPU SolverVBD implementation and records its checkpoints. The fixed triangle is a soft triangle with three zero-mass vertices; this is the branch’s soft self-contact ALM-DAT path.

uv run --script tmp/alm-dat/run_example.py \
  --dt 0.12 --height 0.04 --iterations 2 --contact-gap 0.2 --steps 120 \
  --output tmp/alm-dat/illustrative-real.json

For the Newton viewer, append --viewer gl --speed 0.1 --paused (and use DISPLAY=:1 when needed). It renders accepted substep states; the website shows the recorded internal checkpoints. The viewer has Reset, pause, and single-step controls.

The larger detection gap makes the isotropic motion bound nonbinding for these anchor advances. The actual DAT plane limits all five. Its plane offset plus reserved margin put the allowed point boundary about 2 µm above the physical triangle.
