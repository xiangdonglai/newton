Three moments to compare

The same anchor operation, at different times

The iteration checkpoint and the final rounds both compute a ← Tr(a, y). At a checkpoint, more force solving can follow. During the final rounds, no further trial or multiplier updates occur. The last iteration’s checkpoint is in addition to the two final rounds.

Several anchor advances per iteration would also be possible. That would be a different schedule; it is not the current one illustrated here.

What this example simplifies

We show PRE_INIT, two solver iterations, a checkpoint after each iteration, and two final rounds. Each iteration shows this vertex’s color group and another group that leaves this vertex unchanged. The trial positions are scripted; this page does not calculate ALM forces. For a fixed boundary at zero, each illustrated truncation advances the anchor through 35% of its remaining positive clearance. This deliberately conservative factor keeps the advances visible; it is not the production default. The plane is held fixed instead of illustrating its reconstruction.

The isotropic motion bound is assumed nonbinding and stays centered at r. With PRE_POST_INIT, a second detection after initialization would set r ← a while preserving y. That extra stage is omitted here to isolate the truncation schedule.

This illustrates soft self-contact in the current ALM-DAT implementation. It makes no claim about rigid-soft ALM-DAT support or general non-penetration guarantees.
