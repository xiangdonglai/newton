## Author's comments:

There are a couple of simplifications one can do related to P_near (I think P_far is more or less as simple as it gets).

The way the potential is designed is to have a small extent of the potential for P_near relative to P_far, serving as a guard for edge-edge contact,  with the hope that it is never activated for area contact, e.g., sliding, or point contact with a vertex hitting a face, which are handled by P_far.    At the same time P_near is by far more complex, with the moving and appearing/disappearing quadrature points, and two mollifiers, one for the quadrature points that  "slide off" an edge, and another for parallel edge-edge (which cannot be avoided, at least as long as we use nearest point distance).  Also P_near by construction is not very accurate, ie behavior may change substantially under refinement, but I am not sure how much of a concern it is in newton.

The two simplifications both  are in the mindset is that P_near is a secondary safeguard.
(1) The simplest thing is not to normalize the weights for the whole triangle -- this breaks quadrature approximation correctness under refinement, but keeps differentiability and barrier properties (if the latter is needed).  This eliminates some of the nonzero off-diagonal blocks in the Hessian (as the weights for vertex quadrature points become independent of edges), and generally reduces the cost/complexity of the gradients and Hessians.  By how much I cannot tell.

(2) Another  more radical simplification in the similar spirit is simply use  IPC-style interaction for edge-edge  in P_near, instead of  using the ESP potential.  This is a fundamental departure as it makes the extent of  P_near limited by the minimal edge length (and minimum is taken over all edge length changes in the dynamic simulation), exactly the problem GCP, OGC and ESP  were addressing.  But this restriction only applies to P_near, and at far distances P_far is sufficient.
The caveat is that the setting should naturally not have extreme compression -- e.g., if one can safely assume that edges never change length by more than 50%.  One can also adapt the barrier extent dynamically, but I do not know what strange effects this may have on the dynamics.

I do not know how much impact on performance  (2) may have,  has but it certainly makes implementation simpler, although I guess these days with  AI tools neither matters too much.  But simple implementation typically allows for better optimization.

A remark about cancellation: if you are using a finite b(d), i.e., a penalty not a barrier, this should be fine, but a sufficiently large penalty or an unbounded barrier is problematic, if you directly rely on f.p. cancellation for large numbers. It may lead to very significant spurious forces due to f.p. error.   E.g., if the forces are 1e-10, and the error is 1e-6, you can get 1e4  spurious forces.  So it is advisable to figure out a way to create an exact cancellation mask, eg using 1 instead of b(d), and apply it, even if this increases the cost.

Finally, for rigid  solids,  with well defined inside and outside, this gives you an easy-to-evaluate  smooth SDF-based penalty for a mesh (assuming in the interior the penalty is evaluated as if the distances were negative).  I am not sure how SDF contact is implemented in newton, e.g., in terms of forces for edges, but I assume this type of SDF can be substituted if this makes sense.   In this setting,  (2) for P_near can be made rigorous, as the edge lengths do not change.
Given that there is no need to avoid edge-edge penetration entirely if penalties are used, perhaps for practical purposes
only using P_far, but with more samples per edge,  may be more efficient, than doing all the computations for EE pairs.



## Implementation on Edge-edge term with unnormalized weights (1)

The following describes the moving EE-sample contribution under option (1):
remove triangle-wide weight normalization, but retain the complete ESP near
potential at each sample. The standalone `evaluate_esp_box_cloth.py` evaluates
this energy; it is not yet wired into the solver or its force/Hessian evaluation.

### Edge-sample energy

Consider one direction (as an example), from source surface $A$ to target surface $B$. Let
$f_i$ be a source triangle with rest area $A_i^0$. For each source edge $e$,
$Q(e)$ contains the pairs $(q,\bar e)$ generated by eligible nearby target
edges $\bar e$, where $q$ is the closest point on $e$. These are nearby
quadrature samples; the edges need not be in actual zero-distance contact.

Using the rest-area Jacobian $2A_i^0$, the unnormalized contribution is

$$
P_{\rm EE}^{A\to B}
=
\sum_{f_i\in F_A}2A_i^0
\sum_{e\subset f_i}
\sum_{(q,\bar e)\in Q(e)}
w(q)P_{\rm near}^{B}(q).
$$

Expanding the point-to-surface potential gives

$$
P_{\rm EE}^{A\to B}
=
\sum_{f_i\in F_A}2A_i^0
\sum_{e\subset f_i}
\sum_{(q,\bar e)\in Q(e)}
w(q)
\left[
\sum_{f'\in F_B}b_{\rm near}\!\left(d(q,f')\right)
-\sum_{e'\in E_B^{\rm int}}b_{\rm near}\!\left(d(q,e')\right)
+\sum_{v'\in V_B^{\rm int}}b_{\rm near}\!\left(\|q-v'\|\right)
\right].
$$

Here $F_A,F_B$ are the source and target face sets. $E_B^{\rm int}$ and
$V_B^{\rm int}$ are the target's interior edges and vertices. For a closed
target mesh these include every edge and vertex; for an open mesh, boundary
edge/vertex corrections are omitted, following supplement S4. The function
$b_{\rm near}$ is the positive near-field barrier term, including the minus
sign in the logarithmic barrier definition. Terms outside its support are zero.

The weight is exactly the paper's Eq. (9):

$$
w(q)=\left[S\!\left(d(e,\bar e)\right)\mu(e,\bar e)\right]^k,
\qquad k=1\text{ for the logarithmic barrier}.
$$

The generating edge $\bar e$ and the inner summation edge $e'$ have different
roles. The inner sums include **all contributing target features**, not just
those incident to $\bar e$. Removing normalization does not change this
requirement. Include the reverse contribution $P_{\rm EE}^{B\to A}$ as well;
the fixed-sample near/far contributions also remain.

### Regroup by source edge

For a fixed source–target surface pair, a shared source edge generates the same
samples and evaluates the same target potential for each of its incident
triangles. Without the triangle-dependent denominator, we can regroup exactly:

$$
P_{\rm EE}^{A\to B}
=
\sum_{e\in E_A}
\underbrace{\left(2\sum_{i:\,e\subset f_i}A_i^0\right)}_{
\text{constant incident rest-area factor}}
\sum_{(q,\bar e)\in Q(e)}w(q)P_{\rm near}^{B}(q).
$$

The incident rest-area factor can be precomputed. Thus each unique EE pair can
be processed once, in both directions, without accumulating this term into
separate source-face numerators and denominators. A shared edge's contribution
to all incident triangles is already included in the factor.

### Kernel pseudocode

The following computes one direction, $P_{\rm EE}^{A\to B}$, with one thread
per unique source-edge/target-edge candidate. The neighborhood queries can be
accelerated with BVHs or complete cached neighbor lists.

```text
P_ee = 0

kernel compute_P_ee:
    parallel for each unique (e, e_bar) in BVH edge candidates:
        (q, q_bar) = closest points on (e, e_bar)

        if the pair does not produce an eligible interior sample:
            return

        w_q = weight from Eq. (9)
        if w_q == 0:
            return

        P_q = 0

        for each unique interior target vertex v' within near support of q:
            P_q += b_near(distance(q, v'))

        for each unique interior target edge e' within near support of q:
            P_q -= b_near(distance(q, e'))

        for each unique target face f' within near support of q:
            P_q += b_near(distance(q, f'))

        A_f  = rest area of one face adjacent to e
        A_f' = rest area of the other adjacent face, or 0 at a boundary

        atomic_add(P_ee, 2 * (A_f + A_f') * w_q * P_q)
```

Important details:

- $q$ is a **closest point on $e$**, not an intersection point; $\bar q$ lies
  on $\bar e$. An eligible moving sample has closest points interior to both
  nonparallel edges. Computing the weight first avoids the neighborhood
  evaluation for zero-weight samples.
- The area factor uses faces **adjacent to the source edge**, not spatially
  nearby faces. It can be precomputed from the rest mesh.
- The target-feature loops include every contributing feature within near
  support, not just features incident to $\bar e$. Each feature is counted once.
  For a closed target mesh, every edge and vertex is interior. For an open
  target mesh, boundary edge/vertex corrections are omitted.
- For the complete bidirectional energy, repeat the evaluation at $\bar q$
  against surface $A$, using the rest areas of faces adjacent to $\bar e$.
  Both directional contributions can be computed in the same EE-pair thread.
- The pseudocode expresses the mathematical signed sum. The authors' warning
  about cancellation still applies: an implementation should cancel matching
  feature contributions before evaluating and subtracting large barrier values.
  Removing weight normalization does not resolve floating-point cancellation.

### Version using a triangle BVH for the primitive queries

The three target-feature loops can be implemented with **one triangle-BVH
query around $q$**, rather than separate vertex, edge, and face BVHs. For each
returned triangle, evaluate its face term and its three edge/vertex corrections.
Shared edges and vertices appear in multiple triangles, so precompute these
target-mesh coefficients:

- `edge_coefficient[e'] = 1/2` for an interior manifold edge and `0` for a
  boundary edge.
- `vertex_coefficient[v'] = 1/n_v` for an interior vertex, where $n_v$ is the
  number of its incident target triangles, and `0` for a boundary vertex.

These counts come from the full target-mesh topology, not from the number of
triangles returned by a particular query. Two adjacent triangles contribute
$-\tfrac12 b_{\rm near}$ each for their shared interior edge, giving one
$-b_{\rm near}$ term. Similarly, $n_v$ contributions of
$\tfrac1{n_v}b_{\rm near}$ count an interior vertex once.
**These coefficients are not $w(q)$ and do not reintroduce quadrature
normalization.**

Let `near_cutoff` be the support radius of $b_{\rm near}$, and `padding` a
small conservative allowance for numerical error in the BVH query. For the
same one-direction energy as above:

```text
P_ee = 0

kernel compute_P_ee_bvh:
    parallel for each unique (e, e_bar) in the prepared EE candidate buffer:
        (q, q_bar) = closest points on (e, e_bar)

        if the pair does not produce an eligible interior sample:
            return

        w_q = weight from Eq. (9)
        if w_q == 0:
            return

        extent = near_cutoff + padding
        lower = q - (extent, extent, extent)
        upper = q + (extent, extent, extent)
        P_q = 0

        for each triangle t returned by target_triangle_BVH.query(lower, upper):
            P_q += b_near(distance(q, t))

            for each of t's three edges e':
                P_q -= edge_coefficient[e'] * b_near(distance(q, e'))

            for each of t's three vertices v':
                P_q += vertex_coefficient[v'] * b_near(distance(q, v'))

        A_f  = rest area of one face adjacent to e
        A_f' = rest area of the other adjacent face, or 0 at a boundary

        atomic_add(P_ee, 2 * (A_f + A_f') * w_q * P_q)
```

The BVH returns triangles whose bounding boxes intersect the query box; this
is a conservative candidate set, not an exact distance test. Evaluate distances
to the finite features, with $b_{\rm near}=0$ outside its actual support. The
padding enlarges only the query, not the potential's support.

This query also covers the edge and vertex terms: if a target edge or vertex
is within the support of $q$, all its incident triangles intersect the query
box. Thus all contributions needed for its shared-feature coefficient are
present, assuming conservative bounds and current geometry.

Implementation notes:

- The outer loop reads an already prepared EE candidate buffer; it is not an
  active BVH traversal nested around the inner triangle query. No scan of the
  global contact rows is needed for each $q$.
- Refit deformable BVHs to current geometry, or use bounds expanded sufficiently
  to cover motion since the last refit. For a rigid mesh-local BVH, transform
  the query and account conservatively for shape scaling. All distance
  evaluations must still use consistent physical coordinates.
- For the reverse contribution, repeat the triangle query around $\bar q$
  against surface $A$, and use the rest-area factor of $\bar e$.
- This computes the same mathematical potential as the unique-feature version.
  The shared-feature coefficients prevent double counting, but **do not solve
  floating-point cancellation** between large positive and negative barrier
  terms. The authors' exact-cancellation recommendation still applies.

## Fixed vertex energy from BVH VT/TV pairs

For each returned vertex–triangle pair $(v,f')$, evaluate the face term and
its edge/vertex corrections. VT means a soft vertex against a rigid triangle;
TV means a rigid vertex against a soft triangle. Both evaluate the same formula:

$$
E_{v,f'}=
b(d(v,f'))
-\sum_{e'\subset f'}c_{e'}b(d(v,e'))
+\sum_{v'\in f'}c_{v'}b(\|v-v'\|).
$$

The target-topology coefficients are $c_{e'}=1/2$ for an interior manifold
edge and zero at a boundary; $c_{v'}=1/n_{v'}$ for an interior vertex incident
to $n_{v'}$ triangles and zero at a boundary. These are incidence corrections,
not quadrature weights. All distances are to finite features, and $b$ is zero
outside its support.

Define $A_v=\sum_{f\ni v}A_f^0$, the **sum of incident source rest-triangle
areas**; its lumped vertex area is $A_v/3$. The standard reference triangle
has area $1/2$, so its three equally weighted vertices each have quadrature
weight $1/6$. Each incident source face contributes $2A_f^0(1/6)=A_f^0/3$.
Regrouping by source vertex gives

$$
P_{\rm fixed}^{A\to B}
=\sum_{v\in V_A}\frac{A_v}{3}
\sum_{(v,f')\in\mathrm{BVH}_{\rm VT}}E_{v,f'}.
$$

Use TV rows for the reverse direction. This preserves the earlier prototype's
fixed-vertex weights: BVH pruning changes which features are visited, not the
energy normalization. Both the vertex and EE terms start with the same
$2A_f^0$ rest-area Jacobian. Vertex quadrature includes the additional $1/6$
weight; EE quadrature retains its own Eq. (9) weight $w(q)$ and the prefactor
$2\sum_{f\supset e}A_f^0$. The moving EE contribution is unchanged.

```text
kernel compute_fixed_vertex_energy:
    parallel for each (v, f') in the returned BVH VT/TV rows:
        E_v = b(distance(v, f'))

        for each edge e' of f':
            E_v -= edge_coefficient[e'] * b(distance(v, e'))

        for each vertex v' of f':
            E_v += vertex_coefficient[v'] * b(distance(v, v'))

        atomic_add(P_v, (A_v / 3) * E_v)
```

This is equivalent to the complete point-to-mesh sum when the pipeline returns
all VT/TV pairs inside the barrier support. Any contributing edge or vertex
belongs to triangles whose distance is no greater than its own, so all of its
incident triangles must be included. Use `full_surface_contact_return_unfiltered=True`,
a query threshold at least as large as the support, and no buffer overflow.
Filtered contacts cannot provide this equivalence.

### Direct accumulation and comparison — 2026-10-07

The prototype now evaluates each VT/TV row's signed face, edge, and vertex
terms directly in float32 and atomically adds the result to that source
vertex's `P` and `P_near`. The resulting vertex potentials are multiplied by
$A_v/3$. This takes one kernel pass, with no dense source-vertex × target-feature
array. Each thread visits only its triangle and its three edges/vertices.

The EE sample potential still scans the complete small target mesh. It also
sums barrier terms directly, with no contact-row × target-feature scratch
array. Pruning those queries is separate work. Both kernels launch at the
contact-buffer capacity and check the device-side active count before reading.

The prior float64 version with integer coefficient cancellation is preserved
in commit `2cb7a1566d5160413e8372a8b6fe34ad67421d07`, also recorded at the top
of `evaluate_esp_box_cloth.py`. Direct accumulation avoids its dense allocation;
unlike integer coefficient cancellation, its cancellation can leave numerical
residuals and its atomic sums can depend on thread order.

`esp_numpy_oracle.py` remains an independent float64 reference: it evaluates
the full finite-feature sum and enumerates all edge pairs. Each run compares
`P`, `P_near`, and both directional fixed/EE energies. The float32 tolerances
are `rtol=2e-5, atol=5e-6` for point values and `rtol=2e-5, atol=1e-8` for
integrated energies. Results and measured errors are recorded in `energy_kernels.md`.

### Reusing detection for EE target-triangle lists — 2026-10-07

The [coverage investigation](2026-10-07-ee-triangle-list-reuse.md) finds that,
for separated primitives, unfiltered endpoint–triangle rows plus triangles
incident to returned EE edges can construct complete per-source-edge target
lists. All samples on that edge can reuse the list, without another BVH query
per sample. CPU/CUDA collision-output checks passed; filtered, stale,
insufficient-radius, and piercing cases demonstrate the limits. The report
contains the argument, measured results, and a runnable diagnostic script.
This is an investigated option, not yet a change to the ESP energy kernels.
